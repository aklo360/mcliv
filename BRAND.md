# MCLIV — Brand Bible

**Version 1.0 (locked) · Supersedes all prior brand notes**

This is the single source of truth for the MCLIV brand identity system. Every
designed artifact — website, menu, label, package, garment, invitation, object
tag, social post, room — must be explainable against this document. If a
decision cannot be justified here, it is wrong. Do not veer.

> **One-line operating formula:**
> Söhne typography + Swiss grid + collectible-design-gallery restraint +
> culinary material + governed New York gesture.
>
> **Four words:** Precision / Matter / Gesture / Ritual.
> Every MCLIV artifact contains at least three of these four.

---

## 0. How to use this document

1. Start with **§1 Position** and **§2 Architecture** — they govern everything.
2. Build the **grid and typography first** (§7, §8). No expressive layer is
   added until hierarchy is solved.
3. Treat **§3 Commandments** and **§17 What MCLIV must not become** as hard
   guardrails — they override taste, mood, and trend.
4. For any new surface, write a one-line content brief first (material,
   context, use, audience, ritual, emotional temperature), then design.

## 1. Position

**MCLIV is a creative design studio at the intersection of functional art and
cuisine.** Roman numeral 1154. NYC-based. Founders: John Black (chef/artist) +
Mike Carrera.

MCLIV does not "design for food." It designs **systems of taste** — menus,
objects, tables, vessels, packaging, editions, dinners, installations,
identities, and spaces where culinary culture becomes an artistic medium.

### The thesis: The System and the Spill

A controlled contradiction. **Swiss design is the skeleton; New York abstract
expressionism is the blood.**

- **The System (Swiss):** grid, typography, proportion, restraint, repetition,
  hierarchy. Credibility and objectivity.
- **The Spill (New York):** gesture, residue, pressure, paint, sauce, char,
  ink, collision. Authorship and charge.

Swiss modernism is the operating system. Abstract expressionism is the
interrupting event. Clean without sterile. Artistic without chaotic. Culinary
without restaurant branding. Luxurious without obvious.

### Brand world

MCLIV should feel like a museum catalogue, a chef's mise en place, a downtown
studio wall, a gallery dinner, a collector's object, a product label, and a
ritual — all at once. It must live equally on a tasting menu, a book spine, a
plate edition, a gallery invitation, a website, a garment tag, a steel object,
or a receipt.

### Audience

Design-conscious collectors, art-adjacent consumers, downtown/streetwear-literate
culture that values scarcity and craft. People who **collect**, not consume.

### Philosophy

"Once seen, it cannot be unseen." Everything MCLIV outputs should feel worth
saving.

## 2. Brand architecture — the four layers

Every MCLIV artifact is built from four stacked layers. This scales from a
receipt to a wall installation.

| Layer | Name | Role | Examples |
|-------|------|------|----------|
| 1 | **The Grid** | Rational foundation | layout, columns, spacing, hierarchy, alignment, margin |
| 2 | **The Object** | Functional layer | menu, vessel, label, package, publication, plate, garment, website, spatial graphic |
| 3 | **The Mark** | Expressive rupture | scrape, stain, brush, burn, crop, handwritten note, culinary/photographic residue |
| 4 | **The Archive** | Memory layer | date, place, material, edition number, collaborator, process, ingredient, caption, provenance |

Build them in order: Grid → Object → Mark → Archive. The Mark is added last and
sparingly; the Archive makes the work feel collected.

## 3. The design commandments (non-negotiable)

1. **Content determines form.** The ingredient, object, artist, chef, material,
   or ritual drives the design. Never start with style.
2. **The grid comes first.** No expressive layer until hierarchy is solved.
3. **Clarity before chaos.** Essential information is understood in three seconds.
4. **White space is a material.** Designed silence: tablecloth, gallery wall,
   pause between courses. Never leftover emptiness.
5. **Typography is the voice.** Precise, restrained, optically corrected.
6. **Fewer elements, made stronger.** Reduce until each remaining element feels
   inevitable.
7. **Color must have a job.** Signal, classify, indicate, or carry material
   temperature. Never decoration.
8. **Photography must prove reality.** Material, process, hand, object, residue,
   event. No stock. No fake lifestyle.
9. **Gesture must be earned.** No random brushstrokes — every mark comes from a
   real process.
10. **The mark may break the grid, not the message.** Expression never destroys
    function.
11. **Every object is designed in use.** Held, read, folded, opened, eaten from,
    saved, collected.
12. **No trend-chasing.** Seasonal expression can evolve; the core system endures
    ten years.
13. **Do not imitate the references.** Learn from Richter, Basquiat, JJJJound,
    Lorenzo, Virgil, Ye — build MCLIV's own language.
14. **Design the total environment.** Object, space, table, light, material,
    sound, pace — not just graphics.
15. **Everything must feel collected, not consumed.**

## 4. Logo & wordmark

### Primary wordmark

**`MCLIV`** — uppercase, typographic, grotesk-based (Söhne / Neue Haas
territory), slightly custom-cut. Institutional gallery mark, not a lifestyle
logo. No fork, spoon, plate, brush, chef hat, crown, or food icon.

### The signature strike (locked)

The MCLIV wordmark carries a single **horizontal strike-through rule** across the
letterforms. This is a permanent, intentional brand device — a clean,
disciplined geometric line (redaction / cancellation / edition-stroke), **not** a
brushstroke, not distress, not texture. It is consistent with the archival,
Swiss sensibility and must be preserved on the primary mark. Assets:
`/public/icons/logo.svg`.

### Wordmark traits

- Uppercase only. Tight-but-breathable tracking. Strong horizontal silhouette.
- No decorative ligatures, no expressive distressing in the core mark, no
  permanent paint/brush attached to the logo.
- The strike is the only permitted device on the core wordmark.

### Logo behavior

Treat the logo like a stamp, maker's mark, gallery caption, or edition label.
It may appear: small in a corner; vertically on a spine/label; stamped/debossed;
engraved into metal; on translucent vellum; placed like a museum credit. Used
large **only** when the brand itself is the subject.

### Secondary device — `IV`

The strongest symbol territory is hidden in the name: **`IV`**. A modular marker
for sequence, course number, edition, four-part structure, or vertical divider.
Discovered through use, never forced. The wordmark is the logo; the symbol is
secondary.

## 5. Typography (locked v0.2)

Typography is the brand's emotional engine — but the emotion comes from **scale,
spacing, cropping, material, and gesture**, never from expressive fonts.

### Locked families (production)

| Role | Typeface | Weight / style | Use |
|------|----------|----------------|-----|
| **Display / headers** | **Google Sans Flex** (variable, self-hosted) | ~400–500 | Headings, project/object/event titles, hero captions, statements |
| **Navigation** | **Inter** (self-hosted; Helvetica fallback) | 400 | Primary nav only — a flatter, plainer grotesk than the display face |
| **Body / labels / archive** | **Geist Mono** (variable, self-hosted) | 400 | Reading text, eyebrows, meta, captions, edition #, provenance, codes |
| Editorial serif | *none* | — | The brand uses no serif. |

All faces are open, free, **self-hosted** as variable woff2 in `/public/fonts/`
(`GoogleSansFlex-Variable.woff2`, `GeistMono-Variable.woff2`, plus
`Geist-Variable.woff2` and `Inter-Variable.woff2` as back-pocket swaps). No
license blocker, no CDN dependency. The archival "code" feel is now native —
the body voice *is* the mono.

> **Conceptual lineage (reference only):** the v0.2 report locked Söhne / Söhne
> Mono as the *ideal* (Akzidenz × Helvetica × NYC Subway). Google Sans Flex +
> Geist + Geist Mono are the chosen production realization of that same
> Swiss-grotesk-with-New-York-infrastructure feeling, free and self-hostable.
> Geist's Sans+Mono pairing delivers the dual gallery-voice / archive-voice
> system the bible requires.

**Active selection (v0.7):** Google Sans Flex = headers; **Inter = nav**;
**Geist Mono = body / labels / archive**. A soft display face for titles, a flat
grotesk for navigation, and a technical mono ground for everything read. Geist
(sans) and all-Google-Sans-Flex remain self-hosted back-pocket swaps.

```
--font-display: "Google Sans Flex", system-ui, sans-serif;        /* headers */
--font-nav:     "Inter", "Helvetica Neue", Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif; /* nav */
--font-sans:    "Geist Mono", ui-monospace, "SF Mono", monospace; /* body / labels */
--font-mono:    "Geist Mono", ui-monospace, "SF Mono", monospace; /* archive */
--font-body:    var(--font-sans);
```

### Do not use

No script. No decorative serif. No distressed/fake-handwriting font. No random
monospace. No trendy "SaaS startup" grotesk. No luxury-fashion serif as default.

### Hierarchy & web scale

| Level | Role | Web size | Notes |
|-------|------|----------|-------|
| Display | Monumental / campaign / single word | 80–180px (`clamp(3.5rem, 10vw, 11rem)`) | sparse, architectural, tight leading |
| L1 | Project / object / event title | 28–56px (`clamp(1.75rem, 4vw, 3.5rem)`) | flush-left |
| L2 | Functional title / section | 18–24px | clear, restrained |
| Body | Reading text | 16–18px | generous leading (~1.5–1.7) |
| Label | Section labels / nav | 11–13px | uppercase, tracked |
| Caption | Provenance / archive | 10–12px | mono, museum-label feel |

### Type rules

1. Max **two** families per system; max **three** weights per composition; max
   **four** sizes per surface.
2. **Flush-left, ragged-right** by default. Centering only for ritual,
   invitation, monumentality, or silence.
3. Use **scale contrast aggressively, font variety never.**
4. Never simulate art with a font — the Mark layer does that.
5. Kerning, line length, and leading are always optically corrected.
6. Captions read like gallery labels or kitchen notes.
7. Söhne Mono is a primary identity device, not an afterthought:
   `MCLIV / COURSE 04 / CHARRED ALLIUM / 19:42 / NYC`.

Restrained, material, culinary, functional. **Every color has a job** — signal,
classify, indicate, or carry material temperature. Never decoration.

**80% neutral / 15% material / 5% signal.**

1. Black + bone are the default world.
2. One dominant accent per project. Never a rainbow.
3. Seasonal color is allowed; the core palette does not change.
4. AbEx color may be chaotic **only inside a controlled container** (a cropped
   scan, a single block).
5. Food color comes from **photography first**, not graphics.
6. Metallic, translucent, charred, and stained surfaces count as color.

## 7. Grid & spacing

The grid is architecture — a table, a kitchen station, a gallery floorplan.
Nothing floats; everything aligns, anchors, stacks, cuts, interrupts, or
*intentionally* violates the grid. When something breaks its place, the rupture
must be meaningful.

### Digital grid

- **12-column** master grid.
- **4px** base unit; **8px** primary spacing unit.
- Generous margins; strong vertical rhythm; modular sections.
- Content blocks align to columns.

### Spacing scale (fixed)

`4 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 24 / 32 / 48 / 64 / 96 / 128`

### Print

- 6-col (small) / 12-col (posters, menus, editorial, packaging).
- Baseline grid for text-heavy work. Large default margins.
- Images may bleed; typography stays anchored to the grid.

### Composition rules

1. Every layout begins with the grid; every element aligns to something.
2. **Asymmetry is the default.** Centering is reserved for ritual / silence.
3. Negative space is a primary material.
4. Large type may crop, but must feel intentional.
5. Marks may break the grid, never comprehension.
6. Most important info readable in three seconds; the beautiful can reveal slowly.

## 8. Photography & image direction

Photography is **evidence of making** — documentary, not influencer.

### The five categories (every image belongs to one)

1. **Object documentation** — clean, frontal, archival (plates, vessels,
   editions, packaging, tools, collaborations).
2. **Process documentation** — hands, fire, cutting, scraping, plating, folding,
   stamping.
3. **Residue studies** — stains, burns, oil, sauce trails, graphite, char,
   scraped paint, table marks.
4. **Installation documentation** — full environment: table, wall, light,
   guests, spacing, object placement.
5. **Abstract scans** — high-res scans of material gestures, used as controlled
   art layers.

### Style

Direct or controlled natural light. Shadows allowed. Texture emphasized.
Cropping can be severe. Real backgrounds. **Avoid:** artificial perfection, fake
luxury, restaurant clichés, shallow-DOF "food porn," stock imagery.

### Rules

Every image shows material truth and belongs to one category. Captions read like
an archive. The aftermath can matter as much as the finished plate.

## 9. The abstract-expressionist mark system

The soul of MCLIV — but **governed**. Without the Swiss system, gesture is
decoration; without gesture, the system is sterile.

### Proprietary mark library (real processes only)

Charcoal drag · oil smear · sauce pull · burnt paper edge · knife scrape · wine
stain · ink wash · squeegee pull · flour dust · salt scatter · graphite notation
· heat discoloration · paint overprint · plate residue · table abrasion.

### Mark rules

1. Marks come from real materials; scanned/photographed at high resolution.
2. Catalogued by **source, date, process, project**.
3. May interrupt the grid; never destroy hierarchy.
4. May obscure secondary content; **never primary**.
5. **One major gesture per composition.**
6. Gesture occupies **10–25%** of a functional layout (more only when the piece
   is explicitly art-forward).
7. Cropped with discipline; always related to the project's material/concept.

### The Richter rule

Layer, scrape, reveal, conceal. Chance may create the mark; **design decides
where it lives.**

### The Basquiat rule

Write, mark, code, annotate — but build MCLIV's **own** glyph vocabulary from
cuisine and functional art (flame, vessel, knife, spoon, table, salt, bone,
time, temperature, course, edition, hand, stain, cut, fold, pour, burn, serve).
Never copy crowns/handwriting. Text may be image, but it must mean something.

### The New York rule

Surfaces may feel layered, tagged, pasted, scraped, overpainted — but never
messy by default. **System first. Street second.**

## 10. Material system

Because MCLIV sits between functional art and cuisine, the brand must be
physically rich — materials do as much work as graphics.

- **Paper:** uncoated warm white, cotton stock, newsprint (studies), translucent
  vellum, heavy card (tags), receipt paper (ephemera), archival (editions).
- **Print finishes:** blind deboss, black foil, white-on-black foil, letterpress,
  riso (art editions), screenprint (posters), overprint, stamping, numbering,
  perforation, fold systems.
- **Object materials:** stainless steel, ceramic, glass, wax, linen, charred
  wood, stone, rubber, raw canvas, aluminum, enamel.

**Rules:** never fake texture digitally when real texture is possible; print
should feel collectible; menus saveable; labels archival; packaging an object,
not a wrapper; even the cheapest material is intentional.

## 11. Voice & language

Precise, sparse, sensorial, archival. Avoid lifestyle clichés, restaurant fluff,
and fake art-speak.

**Tone:** exact · calm · observational · material · slightly cryptic · confident.
Never cute, never corporate, never over-explanatory.

**Good MCLIV language**
> "Burnt leek. Bone broth. Black ceramic. Course IV."
> "An edible study in heat, ash, and restraint."
> "Prepared in New York. Served once. Archived permanently."

**Bad MCLIV language (banned)**
> "Elevated culinary experiences for modern tastemakers."
> "Where art meets food in unforgettable ways."
> "Bold, innovative, unique design solutions." · "Luxury vibes."

### Naming system

Direct, coded, archival: `MCLIV STUDY 001` · `TABLE OBJECT 04` · `COURSE IV` ·
`SERVICE 02` · `THE CHAR STUDY` · `DINNER AS OBJECT` · `BONE / STEEL / FIRE` ·
`EDITION 12` · `ROOM FOR SALT`.

## 12. Motion & interaction

Minimal, functional, **Bass-like reduction: one idea, one move.** No trendy
over-animation, no agency tropes.

| Interaction | Behavior |
|-------------|----------|
| Hero | Full-screen image slideshow, slow cross-fade, minimal text overlay (see §13) |
| Transitions | Subtle, purposeful; respect `prefers-reduced-motion` (disable all, opacity 1) |
| Hover | Quiet — image scale or a single rule; nav uses a refined cue, not loud effects |
| Image reveal | Slow, gallery-paced |

Accessibility: `.sr-only` for SR-only content; aria on interactive elements;
focus-visible outlines; `prefers-reduced-motion` honored everywhere.

## 13. Web / digital system

The website is a **digital archive and curated gallery**, not an agency
portfolio. Reference behavior (not aesthetics) from **Carpenters Workshop
Gallery** — image-led, quiet, structured around work / object / artist /
location / exhibition. Quiet because it is confident. "Build an archive that
makes the work look inevitable."

### Homepage hero (locked direction)

**Full-screen image slideshow** with **minimal text overlay** — Carpenters
Workshop Gallery style. Edge-to-edge documentary photography, slow cross-fade,
sparse overlay (wordmark + a single line / project credit in Söhne, optional
Söhne Mono caption). No heavy nav chrome over the image. Let the work carry it.

### Information architecture (target)

Primary nav (sparse): **Work · Objects · Table · Archive · Studio · Contact**
Commerce layer (only where editioned product exists): **Projects · Account · Cart**

> Current site implements `Home · Activations · Shop · Contact` on Shopify
> Hydrogen. Migrate IA toward the target above as content grows; keep real
> Shopify cart/checkout for editions/drops. Per studio notes: take down
> tours/workshops (workshops may return later, outsourced).

### Web rules

- Sparse navigation. Large image fields. Typographic project indexes.
- **Archive mode**: dates, editions, locations, materials, course numbers,
  collaborators as first-class content.
- Object pages, collaboration pages, journal / field notes.
- Captions matter. Motion subtle and purposeful. No over-animated tropes.

### Web hierarchy (priority order)

1. Typography (Söhne / Söhne Mono) defines the voice.
2. Image — large, quiet, object-led.
3. Archive — dates, editions, locations, materials, course numbers.
4. Gesture — AbEx marks as controlled ruptures only.
5. Commerce — present, but never a normal retail feel.

## 14. Applications (quick reference)

- **Menus:** Swiss poster × gallery checklist × chef's prep sheet. Flush-left,
  course numbers, ingredient hierarchy, optional timestamps/material notes,
  sparse mark, archival captions, generous margins. No food icons, no script.
  `COURSE 03 / CHARRED LEEK · BLACK GARLIC · BONE BROTH / fire · allium · smoke · 19:42`
- **Packaging:** editioned object. Label first; batch #, material/ingredient
  statement, small mark, strong negative space, one tactile finish, one
  controlled mark.
- **Social:** an output channel, not the brand. Post sequences, repetition,
  project codes; alternate object / process / residue / final. Preserve mystery.
- **Apparel:** studio uniform, not merch. Heavy blanks, minimal placement, small
  labels, edition codes, washed neutrals, occasional large back type.
- **Spatial:** gallery logic — labels at consistent height, object spacing,
  menus aligned to place settings, lighting + pacing as identity. The table is a
  grid.

## 17. What MCLIV must not become

- A generic luxury food brand. · A minimalist restaurant identity. · A fake
  gallery brand. · A black-and-white Helvetica cliché. · A Basquiat or Virgil
  imitation. · A JJJJound moodboard with no point of view. · A streetwear brand
  with food references. · A studio that looks like every other studio. · A
  chaotic art brand with no system. · A sterile Swiss brand with no blood.

The danger is not being too strange — it is being **tasteful but conceptually
weak.** The system must have teeth.

- **Identity:** `MCLIV` uppercase grotesk wordmark + signature strike; `IV`
  secondary device. No icon unless system-generated later.

- **Imagery:** documentary, material-first, process-heavy. No stock.
- **Marks:** real process only, scanned + catalogued, one major gesture per
  layout, never compromise primary info.
- **Voice:** sparse, archival, material, exact. No fluff.
- **Motion:** minimal, functional, one idea one move.
- **Web:** image-led gallery archive; full-screen slideshow hero with minimal
  overlay; sparse nav; archive behavior; commerce last.

**MCLIV is the grid, the object, the mark, and the ritual.** Precise enough to be
trusted, tactile enough to be desired, expressive enough to be remembered.
